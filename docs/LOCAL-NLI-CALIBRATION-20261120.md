# Local NLI calibration — 20 November 2026

This is a small **authored synthetic** semantic-relation test, not an independently annotated corpus, real-world misinformation benchmark or classifier training set. The fixtures and their provenance are included in `backend-api/tests/fixtures/local_nli_pairs.jsonl`. They never enter DistilBERT-LR training.

The article excerpt is the premise; the claim is the hypothesis. All three model labels are read by their names, not by an assumed array order. Inference uses the pinned local multilingual mDeBERTa snapshot, FP32 on CPU, with no remote inference or truncation of a material claim. See the [model author's model card](https://huggingface.co/MoritzLaurer/mDeBERTa-v3-base-mnli-xnli). Multilingual capability is not a verified Taglish guarantee.

The 36 authored scenarios have three relationships each. Twenty-four scenarios (72 pairs) select thresholds; 12 different scenarios (36 pairs) are reserved for audit. Threshold selection maximizes accepted correct relations with zero wrong accepted calibration relations; ties prefer a higher threshold. An insufficient safe threshold would disable that relation at 1.0. Both the ordinary grid and a finer upper-tail grid are recorded. The initial grid stopped at 0.995 and could not find a safe acceptance threshold; extending that declared diagnostic sweep into the upper tail produced 0.998 entailment / 0.999 contradiction. The audit never selected the cutoffs.

Calibration: 20 of 48 support/contradiction pairs accepted correctly, 28 left related; all 24 unrelated pairs left related. Audit: 6 of 24 support/contradiction pairs accepted correctly, 18 left related; all 12 unrelated pairs left related. Three-way audit accuracy is **50%**, driven by abstention to related. Raw model scores sometimes confidently confused Tagalog/Taglish negation and unrelated events. These cutoffs prioritize avoiding wrong overrides at the expense of coverage. They do not prove calibrated probability, factual correctness or a useful false-positive rate on Facebook.

The JSON record includes per-pair outputs, fixed group splits, source-file SHA-256, confusion matrices by language and every swept threshold. Reusing stored scores checks the fixture hash and model revision and avoids unnecessary repeated inference. The original 108-pair inference took about 20.955 seconds; the later threshold resweep from those saved scores is not an inference-speed benchmark.

Runtime additionally requires readable article text, an actual body passage, preserved material anchors and the configured publisher/authority policy. It may reject a relation accepted in this isolated score test. Headlines, search snippets, unsupported quotes and missing evidence cannot become a fabricated evidence override. A representative independently reviewed claim/article set is still needed before stronger reliability claims.
